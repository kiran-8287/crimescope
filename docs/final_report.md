# CrimeScope — Final Report

## Title

**CrimeScope: a reproducible, provenance-verified analytics platform for
district-level reported-crime patterns in India (2021–2024).**

## Abstract

CrimeScope is an end-to-end analytics platform for **reported** (registered) crime
patterns in India, built on the National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB) *Crime in India*
district-wise IPC/BNS tables for **2021–2024**. It ingests and cleans 144 source
fragments into a canonical, traceable observation table, publishes an immutable
SHA-256-verified SQLite release, derives a versioned analysis run, and serves both a
web dashboard and a reproducible research-findings script. The active release
(`ncrb-districtwise-2021-2024-ecec48b772bc`) contains **567,078** canonical rows
(**546,198** eligible, **20,880** aggregate) across years 2021–2024. The active
analysis run (`ncrb-national-2024-admin-log1p-zscore-20261107-v4`) uses the 2024
national cohort of **774** administrative-district reporting units × **131** category
features. Five investigations report verified, artifact-derived results: PC1 is a
reporting-volume axis (corr with log1p(total) = −0.88; PC1+PC2 retain 20.48 % of
variance); the 2022→2023 annual rise (+4.70 %) reduces to **+3.15 %** on the 749
matched units; the k=3 clusters separate consistent profile groups; a near-zero-baseline
rare category and a genuine high-volume outlier are reproduced exactly under the
sample-std convention; and three non-tautological district-level association rules are
recomputed from the transaction matrix. Every figure is generated by one entry point,
[`scripts/research_findings.py`](../scripts/research_findings.py), which fails closed on
any dataset/artifact hash mismatch. All results are descriptive: reported counts are not
all crime, no rates or safety rankings are produced, and no causal claims are made.

## 1. Problem statement and research questions

District-level crime data in India is published as large, heterogeneous spreadsheets with
tiered headers, changing law frameworks and changing administrative geographies. Naive
use produces double-counting (parent totals overlapping their components), spurious
2023→2024 trends across an IPC→BNS reclassification, and comparisons between
non-equivalent reporting units. CrimeScope addresses this by making provenance and
comparability first-class, and by answering five research questions with reproducible
arithmetic:

1. **Composition** — What types of crime are reported in a selected region?
2. **Comparison** — How does a region compare with other compatible regions?
3. **Trend** — How have comparable records changed over time?
4. **Structure** — Which regions have similar profiles, and what explains the similarity?
5. **Anomalies** — Which unusual observations deserve investigation?

## 3. Dataset provenance and active scope

- **Source:** NCRB *Crime in India* district-wise IPC and BNS tables. The active
  2021–2024 fragments are available through `python scripts/download_data.py`; source
  identity and checksums are recorded in its ignored download manifest.
- **Active scope:** years **2021–2024**, `36 state/UT fragments`; 2020 is **excluded**
  because the only available 2020 input covered a partial Andhra Pradesh fragment. The
  partial-2020 files are in the external cleanup archive and are not served or analysed.
- **Active release:** `ncrb-districtwise-2021-2024-ecec48b772bc`
  (dataset sha256 `ecec48b772bc…`, SQLite sha256 `a030e5e01f51…`): 567,078 rows,
  546,198 eligible, 20,880 aggregate, 144 source files.
- **Active run:** `ncrb-national-2024-admin-log1p-zscore-20261107-v4`, 2024 cohort,
  774 administrative-district reporting units × 131 features.
- **Pointer files:** `data/serving/current.json`, `analysis/output/runs/current.json`.
  The backend refuses (fail-closed) to serve a run whose dataset hash differs from the
  served release, and re-hashes the feature input and every artifact before use.

Reporting-unit type is `administrative_district`, `city_police`, `other_police_unit`,
`railway`, or `aggregate`. Administrative districts are classified **partly by pattern
inference**; the 774-unit set is a *reporting* cohort and is not claimed to be an
independently verified administrative list.

## 4. Preparation pipeline, methods and parameter choices

**Pipeline.** Manifest-selected raw fragments → phase-C canonical build
(`crime_observations`) → staged SQLite candidate → separately selected serving release →
analysis run. Each stage records row accounting and hashes; see
[`methodology.md`](methodology.md) for source, cleaning, and classification decisions.

**Feature construction.** For the 2024 cohort the matrix uses source **leaf and component**
categories only — official totals and parent totals are excluded to avoid overlapping
sums. Each reported count is transformed `log1p(x)` and each feature is standardized with
**population** std (`ddof=0`); zero-variance features are left as zero.

**Parameters (from the run manifest).** PCA: 2 components, SVD, explained variance divided
by total feature variance. K-means: `seed = 42`, k ∈ {3, 4, 5}, K-means++ init. Anomaly
detection: cross-sectional `z = (value − mean) / std` with **sample** std (`ddof=1`),
threshold `|z| ≥ 2.5`, skipped when a category has fewer than 3 values or zero std.
Association rules: Apriori, item present at count **≥ 10**, `min_support 0.15`,
`min_confidence 0.6`, `min_lift 1.05`, `max_itemset_size 2` → 739 qualifying transactions
and 697 saved rules.

## 5. Five findings (verified)

Full detail, figures and reproduction: [`docs/findings.md`](findings.md).

1. **Reporting volume and PCA.** Joining the saved PCA scores to the official 2024 total
   (774 joined, **0 unmatched**) gives `corr(PC1, log1p total) = −0.877`; PC1 loads
   strongly and uniformly on the largest categories (Vehicle Theft −0.854, Murder −0.804),
   so it is a volume axis. PC1+PC2 retain **20.48 %** of the 131-dimensional variance, so
   the 2-D plot is a summary, not a faithful similarity map.
2. **Annual totals vs matched-unit change.** Annual reported totals are 2,909,532 (2021),
   2,808,408 (2022), 2,940,460 (2023), 2,740,258 (2024). The 2023-vs-2022 annual change is
   **+4.70 %**; on the **749** matched units it is **+3.15 %**. Both years reconcile as
   `annual = matched + excluded`. Name matching is operational, not boundary continuity.
3. **Cluster structure.** Saved k=3 gives **C1 = 190** units, **C2 = 168** units and
   **C3 = 416** units; medians of the official total are 5,309 (C1) / 159.5 (C2) /
   2,974.5 (C3). **C1 (n=190)** is centroid-high on Stalking (1.04) and Day Burglary
   (1.04); **C2 (n=168, the smallest)** is centroid-low on Murder (−1.47) and Vehicle
   Theft (−1.41); **C3 (n=416, the largest)** is a broad mid-volume group with a flatter
   profile. Inertia is reported for k=3/4/5 but **no optimal k is declared** (inertia
   falls mechanically). Cluster membership in this run is a similarity statement in the
   standardized feature space only — it does not isolate crime composition from
   reporting volume, is not a safe/unsafe or urban/rural classification, the fixed seed
   is a reproducibility convention rather than a stability guarantee, and the lower
   inertia at higher k is not evidence that a larger k is the best model.
4. **Rare and high-volume flags.** The largest-|z| rare flag is *Abetment outside India*
   in Satara, Maharashtra (value 1, 773/774 zeros), reproduced z = **27.7849** = saved;
   the largest-value high-volume flag is *Other Thefts/305/307* in North-West, Delhi
   (value 13,156), reproduced z = **10.8875** = saved. Both use the sample-std (ddof=1)
   convention. A flag is not an error or a risk score.
5. **Association rules.** From N = **739** districts (item present at count ≥ 10), three
   non-tautological single-item rules were recomputed exactly: Stalking → Arson
   (lift 2.27), Stalking → Sexual-Harassment "In Any other place" (lift 2.26), Dowry Death
   → Kidnapping to compel marriage (lift 1.93). These are district-level co-occurrences,
   not shared incidents or causation.

## 6. Dashboard functionality

| Route | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `/` Overview | Reported totals, top categories/regions, drill-down drawer |
| `/compare` Regional Comparison | Multi-unit chip selector, profile comparison, factual summary |
| `/trends` Yearly Trends | Annual totals, verified 2022–2023 matched cohort, single-region history |
| `/quality` Data Quality & Coverage | Source reconciliation, coverage history, limitation register |
| `/pca` PCA Explorer | Two-component variance bars + remaining variance, PCA score plot (PC1–PC2), contextual loadings |
| `/clusters` Cluster Profiles | k=3/4/5 sizes, standardized feature deviations, representatives |
| `/anomalies` Unusual Observations | Rare-category vs high-volume tabs, sample-std note |
| `/rules` Association Rules | Paginated rules with a single pagination owner, interpretation drawer |
| `/methodology` Methodology & Provenance | Live release/run hashes and answered research questions |

Contextual category labels (parent › leaf) are re-attached from verified source metadata by
stable id + year. CSV exports carry contextual columns (category id, source label, display
label, year, reporting-unit id/name/state/type, release). Table exports re-fetch the full
matching set (not just the visible page) and escape commas, quotes, line breaks and
formula-injection prefixes.

## 8. Limitations

1. **Reported ≠ actual crime.** Registered counts reflect reporting behaviour and police
   recording practice; no statement about true prevalence or safety is made.
2. **No population denominators.** Rates per capita are omitted (no validated denominator).
3. **Partial inference.** Administrative-district classification and name matching are
   operational; they are not proof of boundary continuity.
4. **IPC→BNS reclassification.** 2023–2024 totals are not compared across the law change.
5. **Overlapping categories.** Totals and their components overlap; composition shares are
   disabled because no mutually exclusive denominator exists.
6. **Single 2024 cohort for structure.** PCA/K-means/anomalies describe 2024 only.
7. **Association population.** The ≥ 10 item threshold drops low-count districts; the 739
   transaction population is a documented scope choice.
8. **Statistical flags.** Flags are unusual values, not errors, wrongdoing or risk.

## 9. Conclusion

CrimeScope demonstrates a complete, provenance-verified analytics pipeline — ingestion,
cleaning, descriptive and bivariate statistics, PCA, K-means and hierarchical clustering,
anomaly detection, and association-rule mining — behind a dashboard that answers five
research questions with reproducible numbers. The emphasis throughout is on honest scope:
reported counts, no safety rankings, no rates without denominators, no cross-
reclassification trends, and no causal or incident-level claims from district-level
co-occurrence.
