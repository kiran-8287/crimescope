Project Purpose: Answering Five Core Research Questions

How CrimeScope translates NCRB reporting tables into structured answers.

Question 1
What types of crime are reported in a selected region?

Answered via the Overview and Regional Details drawer, displaying recorded counts across individual leaf and component crime categories without false additive composition.

Question 2
How does that region compare with other compatible regions?

Answered via Regional Comparison, allowing side-by-side evaluation of up to 3 compatible reporting units across primary and multi-category profiles, with warnings when comparing different unit types.

Question 3
How have comparable records changed over time?

Answered via Yearly Trends, separating annual volume totals, name-matched pairwise cohorts (e.g. 749 matched 2022–2023 districts), and single-region historical trajectories.

Question 4
Which regions have similar crime profiles, and what explains those similarities?

Answered via PCA Explorer and Cluster Profiles, projecting profiles into two principal components and grouping units via K-means into distinguishable volume and crime-type clusters.

Question 5
Which unusual observations deserve investigation?

Answered via Unusual Observations, screening for cross-sectional outliers (|z| ≥ 2.5) and contextualizing rare-category occurrences against higher-count anomalies.

Course Methods Implemented in CrimeScope

Each method is tagged as dashboard-connected (computed into the active run and served on a page) or an offline utility.

Data Ingestion & Canonical Cleaning
Dashboard-connected
01_cleaning

Ingests 144 raw CSV fragments into a unified schema, separates 20,880 aggregate total rows from reporting-unit observations, preserves explicit reported zeros distinct from true missing/null cells, and quarantines malformed keys.

Descriptive Analytics & Trends
Dashboard-connected
02_descriptive

Computes volume distributions and name-matched pairwise cohorts without unsupported cross-framework continuous line joins across the 2023 IPC to 2024 BNS transition.

Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
Dashboard-connected
05_pca

Decomposes the log1p, population-standardized (ddof = 0) feature matrix across 774 administrative districts using SVD. PC1 captures 15.9% variance (a reporting-volume axis); PC2 captures 4.6% variance (a secondary contrast between bodily/dowry offences and public-order offences). Reported loadings are scaled component loadings (v_ij × √λ_i), not exact Pearson correlations.

K-Means Clustering
Dashboard-connected
06_kmeans

Groups units into k = 3, 4 and 5 clusters by Euclidean distance in the same standardized profile space. The run presents k = 3 as its selected configuration; each cluster is described from its saved centroid and member sizes rather than a fixed narrative.

State-Scoped Hierarchical Clustering (Ward)
Dashboard-connected
07_hierarchical

Applies agglomerative Ward clustering within individual state cohorts to avoid cubic complexity across the national matrix and to keep each state’s jurisdiction grouping intact.

Statistical Anomaly Screening (cross-sectional)
Dashboard-connected
08_anomalies

Computes per-category z-scores within the 2024 cross-sectional cohort using the raw-count sample standard deviation (ddof = 1). Separates rare-category indicators from higher-count outliers. This is the additional sixth functionality beyond the five core methods.

Association Rule Mining (Apriori)
Dashboard-connected
09_association

Mines frequent category co-occurrence across 739 qualifying district transactions (a category counts as present at ≥ 10 cases). Evaluates support, confidence and lift over the frozen national transaction population.

Year-on-year (temporal) anomaly detection
Offline utility
anomaly · year_on_year

The pipeline also produces year-over-year percentage-change flags, but the dashboard serves only the 2024 cross-sectional flags. The temporal output is an offline artifact and is not loaded by any page.

Bivariate category correlation
Offline utility
bivariate

Computes pairwise category correlation matrices as an offline utility. These correlations are not exposed on any page; the Rules page shows association rules instead.

Aggregates are excluded from district sums and used only for reconciliation.	20,880 aggregate observations distinguished from 5,46,198 eligible non-aggregate observations (Quality page reconciliation).
A blank source cell and a reported 0 are different facts.	Preserve explicit zeros as 0 and keep genuine blanks as null; never impute or zero-fill.	Zeros and missing values remain distinguishable in every table and export.	Quality page separates "Explicit reported zeros" from "Missing / null values" (0 retained nulls in the active release).
Source category rows overlap; no verified mutually-exclusive category mapping exists.	Disable composition/shares and forbid additive totals across categories.	No pie charts or "top categories total" sums are presented.	categoryDisplay.compositionSharesAvailable = false — "No complete validated mutually-exclusive category mapping exists across these source tables; category shares are disabled.".
Source rows name police jurisdictions, not a gazetteer-verified district list.	Classify the 774 units as administrative districts with partly inferred classification.	Unit type is retained and labelled as inferred rather than verified.	774 units × 131 features; the unit-type badge marks inferred classification.
Cross-year unit identity must be inferred because names and boundaries can change.	Match units by state plus name, for the 2022–2023 cohort only.	Matched change is reported separately from annual change, with distinct denominators.	749 matched units; 1 unit (2022) and 21 units (2023) excluded (Trends reconciliation).

Distance-based methods operate on relative profile shape, not raw volume.	PCA/clustering feature mode = log1p_zscore; anomaly screening instead uses raw counts with the ddof = 1 sample standard deviation.

Source Archive vs Original Publisher

The dashboard reads a locally staged archive assembled from the NCRB district-wise source tables (144 source fragments; 5,67,078 canonical observations). The SHA-256 hashes in the provenance details above prove that the exact staged artifact recorded in the active release was read and that it has not been altered since publication.

A hash proves artifact integrity, not correct interpretation. It does not guarantee that the original NCRB publication was transcribed without error, that every published table or field was acquired, or that reporting boundaries were unchanged across years. Those remain documented limitations of the source data.

Jurisdictional Classification Rules

In NCRB source publications, reporting units represent police jurisdictions rather than uniform revenue districts. CrimeScope categorizes each unit into:

Administrative Districts: Revenue districts with coterminous district police commands.
City Police / Commissionerates: Metropolitan police jurisdictions (e.g. Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi, DCP Cuttack) carving out dense urban centers.
Railway Police (GRP / SRP): Specialized jurisdictions operating along railway infrastructure across districts.
Specialized / Rural Units: Cyber crime ranges, ATS, crime branches, and rural police divisions (e.g. Pune Rural, Ahmedabad Rural).
Key Limitations & Safe Interpretation
No Population Denominators: NCRB district tables do not provide annual resident population denominators. Therefore, recorded counts reflect volume, not per-capita crime rates.
Reporting & Policing Intensity: Higher counts often reflect vigorous police recording, proactive enforcement (e.g. liquor/narcotics seizures), or higher citizen reporting trust, not higher victimization.
No Safety Rankings: Jurisdictions are never categorized as "safe" or "unsafe" from raw counts alone.
Legal Transition 2023–2024: India transitioned from IPC to BNS in July 2024. Cross-year continuous lines across this boundary are intentionally withheld.
CrimeScope is an academic analytics project adhering strictly to reproducible, evidence-backed pipelines. All claims derive deterministically from canonical NCRB source tables without synthetic imputation.
