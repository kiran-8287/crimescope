Mathematical Note: Why 1 Case Can Produce a Z-Score of ~27.78

In a national cohort of N = 774 administrative districts, if 773 districts report 0 cases and exactly 1 district reports 1 case, the cohort mean is μ = 1/N ≈ 0.00129 and the standard deviation (sample, n−1 denominator) is σ = √(1/N) ≈ 0.0359. The resulting z-score is z = (1 − μ) / σ = (N − 1)/√N ≈ 27.78. Thus, extreme z-scores with small positive counts are mathematically expected for sparse categories and reflect category rarity rather than high crime volume.
